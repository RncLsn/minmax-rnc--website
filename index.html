What long sequences demand

To process truly long sequences, a model needs four properties at once, and getting all four together is hard.

High Expressivity

with long-term memory

Remember what matters for as long as needed, and recognise rich temporal patterns: from simple ones, like “B happened after A”, up to everything a finite-memory system can recognise.

Computational Efficiency

Subquadratic parallelism & Constant cost per token

Cost grows only linearly with length. Run it step by step, or in parallel with a number of sequential steps that grows only logarithmically.

Guaranteed Stability

Independent of sequence length

Activations and gradients stay bounded, however long the sequence gets.

Long-Range Sensitivity

Non-vanishing gradients

Early inputs can still shape late outputs, so dependencies across very long distances can be learned.

Today’s architectures trade them off

Each popular family gives up at least one of the four. MinMax RNCs have all four at once. See how ↓

The idea: swap + and × for max and min

Recurrent networks update their state with sums and products. MinMax RNCs use max and min instead.

MinMax Recurrent Neurons

Each neuron keeps a state x and, at every step, reads two numbers computed from the current input: a cap R and a floor s. It updates its state like this:

xt=max(min(Rt,xt−1),st)
xt=(Rt⊗xt−1)⊕st

Two notations for the same update. In the MinMax algebra, ⊕ and ⊗ play the roles of sum and product: a⊕b=max(a,b) and a⊗b=min(a,b).

In words: keep the old state, but no higher than the cap; then raise it to at least the floor. This is the familiar linear recurrence xt=Rt×xt−1+st, with × replaced by min and + by max.

The state is written once (step 1), held unchanged while the caps stay above it (steps 2 and 3), and overwritten when a low cap resets it (step 4). This kind of exact, lossless holding is what lets the model remember over arbitrarily long spans.

In general, each neuron keeps a state xt with d entries and, at every step, reads from the current input a d×d cap matrix Rt and a floor vector st with d entries. It updates its state like this:

xt,i=max(max1≤j≤dmin(Rt,ij,xt−1,j),st,i)
xt=(Rt⊗xt−1)⊕st

Two notations for the same update. In the MinMax algebra, ⊕ is max and ⊗ is min, so Rt⊗xt−1 is the usual matrix–vector product with max in place of sums and min in place of products.

In words: entry i of the new state is the largest of the old entries, each first capped by its entry of Rt, and at least the floor. This is the linear recurrence xt=Rtxt−1+st with × replaced by min and + by max; with d=1 it is the scalar update.

Try it: a two-entry state that tracks parity

This neuron reads one number u per step, with cap R(u)=(1−uuu1−u) and floor s=(00), starting from state x0=(10). So R(0) is the identity and R(1) swaps the two entries.

Press Step to run it one input at a time. You can also edit the inputs.

StepInput uUpdateState
1(0110)⊗(10)⊕(00)=(01)(01)
2(1001)⊗(01)⊕(00)=(01)(01)
3(0110)⊗(01)⊕(00)=(10)(10)

The neuron swaps its two state entries on input 1 and leaves them unchanged on input 0. Its state is (10) after an even number of 1s and (01) after an odd number: it implements parity. Swapping two entries needs a state of dimension two. In general, the state dimension of a neuron matches the degree of the permutations it can realise, and a single number already suffices for the star-free patterns.

Architecture of MinMax RNCs

A MinMax RNC is a cascade of layers of such neurons, where each neuron’s cap and floor are computed from the input by a small feedforward network.

Why MinMax works

Max and min are simple, but they give MinMax RNCs all four properties, with guarantees.

✓ Maximal expressivity

MinMax neurons can implement all the basic building blocks of finite-state machines, so MinMax RNCs capture all regular languages. Neurons with a single number as their state already capture the star-free ones.

✓ Efficient, step by step or in parallel

Updates can be run one step at a time, like any recurrent network. They also compose associatively, so a whole sequence can be processed with a parallel scan.

✓ Guaranteed stability

Max and min only ever select one of their inputs; they never amplify. States and activations stay bounded at every length, and so do the gradients used in training.

✓ Long-range sensitivity

A neuron can carry its state unchanged, or permuted, across any number of steps. Gradients between distant states can then keep full strength, instead of vanishing.
